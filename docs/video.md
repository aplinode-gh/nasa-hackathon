## 1. Challenge Summary

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Challenge name | Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data |
| Event | 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge |
| Challenge author | Satie Bernett Budro (NASA Johnson Space Center) |
| Core question | What could come next for farms as environmental conditions change? |
| Primary input | NASA public Earth observation data |
| Primary output | A tool that helps explore future farming options and their tradeoffs |
| Closing prompt | "What will you create?" |

**In one sentence:** Participants build a tool, using NASA's public Earth observation data, that helps farmers and decision makers explore what could come next as conditions on the ground change.

## 2. Narrative and Context

The challenge opens with a deliberate shift in perspective:

1. **Zoom out.** In a universe filled with worlds, Earth is the one we understand better than any other.
2. **Look closer.** Even on Earth, the conditions beneath our feet are changing.
3. **Bring it home.** We do not need to travel to another world to find a challenge worthy of exploration. The mission is here.

This framing connects space-agency expertise in observation and remote sensing to a ground-level, human problem: agriculture under changing conditions.

## 3. The Problem

The transcript names the following changes affecting farms:

| Change | Description (as stated) |
|---|---|
| Heat | Heat shifts |
| Rainfall | Rainfall changes |
| Water | Water becomes less certain |
| Soil | Soil loses nutrients |

**The decision pressure:** Farmers must decide what comes next. The challenge centers on supporting that decision under uncertainty, not on describing the changes alone.

---

## 4. The Mission

> Turn NASA's Earth observations into a tool that helps explore what could come next.

Stated framing of the data: Earth is constantly sending signals, and NASA's public Earth observation data gives us new ways to listen. The observations are described as different measurements at different scales that are pieces of the same story.

The mission has three parts:

1. **Listen:** Use public NASA Earth observation data to read the signals.
2. **Connect:** Combine observations across types and scales into one coherent picture.
3. **Enable decisions:** Produce a tool that supports choices about what comes next on the farm.

## 5. Data Sources and Observation Types

The transcript lists five observation categories:

| Observation | Role in the story |
|---|---|
| Rainfall | Water input and variability |
| Soil moisture | Water available to crops at ground level |
| Heat | Temperature stress and seasonal shifts |
| Vegetation | Crop and land condition |
| Water | Broader water availability and certainty |

**Important note from the narration:** these are different observations at different scales. A good solution must account for scale differences (for example, resolution, coverage and revisit frequency) when combining them.

## 6. Expected Capabilities of a Solution

The mission statement lists four capabilities that a strong submission should support:

| Capability | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Compare rotations | Let users evaluate different crop rotation options side by side |
| Reveal tradeoffs | Show what is gained and lost with each option (for example, yield versus water use) |
| Understand risk | Express the likelihood and impact of unfavorable outcomes |
| Make uncertainty visible | Show confidence ranges and data limitations, not only single-point answers |

---

## 7. Solution Formats

The challenge is open on format. Named examples:

| Format | Description |
|---|---|
| AI model | A predictive or analytical model built on the observation data |
| Interactive map | A spatial interface for exploring conditions and options |
| Visualization | A visual explanation of trends, scenarios or risk |
| Decision support tool | A guided tool that helps a farmer or planner choose between options |
| Something new | The narration explicitly invites ideas "none of us has imagined yet" |

The formats are not mutually exclusive. A decision support tool can include a map, a model and visualizations together.

## 9. Key Themes

1. **Earth as a frontier.** The most familiar planet still holds problems worth exploring.
2. **Observation to action.** Data is valuable when it informs a decision.
3. **Honest uncertainty.** Showing what we do not know is part of the deliverable.
4. **Integration across scales.** Different datasets must be fused into one story.
5. **Open creativity.** No fixed format is prescribed.
6. **Human focus.** The end user is a farmer facing real choices.

## 10. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Earth observation | Measuring Earth's surface and atmosphere from space or aircraft |
| Remote sensing | Gathering information about Earth without direct contact, usually via satellites or sensors |
| Crop rotation | Planting different crops in sequence on the same land |
| Soil moisture | Water content held in soil |
| Decision support tool | Software that helps a user compare options and choose |
| Uncertainty | The range of possible outcomes or error around an estimate |
